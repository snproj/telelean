# Slice 5 plan: references (spec v4.1)

*Integrator's plan, 2026-11-20, written on autopilot (the user is away and asked for the slices to be built without stopping; `verbatim_prompt_log/bundle_21/user_requested_documents/02_autopilot_decisions.md` logs every choice made without them). The specification is `verbatim_prompt_log/bundle_21/user_requested_documents/01_spec_v4.md` (§13, CF-1, CM-4, FZ-4, S12, ES-9); clause IDs refer to it. The user's own words outrank everything here: REFERENCE SYSTEM 1 (`bundle_20/prompt20.md`: "reference another card, or any of its fields, clearly and conveniently … a dropdown menu upon typing a symbol like "#"") and ★D6, "search by sentence" (`bundle_21/prompt21_midturn.md`, message 1). One design fed this plan (references); it was checked against the two slice-4 designs (the transform engine, the transform window), and §7.1 resolves their conflicts. Ultra review rounds 1 and 2 revised the plan; **AP-31 then simplified it together with slice 4's** (§8, 2026-11-20): telelean never rewrites a reference, a frozen card's holds never move, no docstring mirror is rewritten inside a session, nothing another subject relies on changes in a session, and one predicate says what Claude may change (the judge's R1–R8 and open items, `scratchpad/plans/simplify45_judged.json`). Slice 4's plan as AP-31 rewrote it (`scratchpad/plans/slice4-plan.md`) is the interface every (S4) mark below refers to. The design before AP-31 is kept as `scratchpad/plans/slice5-plan.after_ultra.md` (and in `subagent_reports/slice5_ultra_review_workflow/`), which every reversal below points to. Every choice the spec leaves open, and every narrowing of it, is in §7.2 as an "AUTOPILOT DECISION" with its alternative and reversal, cited as (D5-n). Slices 2–4 are cited by their decisions, (D2-n), (D3-n), (D4-n); their built contracts are the interface this plan builds on. The coverage audit's "Scope of slices 4 and 5" (15 rows, plus the slice-4 rows that need references first) is mapped row by row in §9.*

*Designed for the simplest sound mechanism (the lesson of AP-24 and AP-31). A reference is a token of text inside the sentence or Why that holds it; nothing about it is stored anywhere else, and only the person's own edit of that field changes it. Holding (S12) adds keys to slice 3's one table and one whole-state check, and to slice 4's one predicate for what Claude may change. Nothing is repaired or revalued, and nothing parallels slices 2–4.*

**Slice 5 = ★D8 (a) item 5, references:**
- **Typing `#`** in a prose field opens a dropdown that finds cards and vocabulary by their sentences and names (RF-1, RF-4, ★D6); → shows a card's fields. The pick is stored as a token, `#[card:ID]`, `#[card:ID:FIELD]`, `#[lean:NAME]` or `#[lean:NAME:FIELD]` (RF-2 a), and is shown everywhere as a chip with the target's current name, or "deleted card" (RF-1 b, RF-2 c). Esc leaves a plain `#`; Lean fields never open it (RF-3).
- **Ids are never reused** (RF-2 b): a per-subject registry, `ids.json`, of every card id telelean has given, or seen in a subject's files when it wrote them; one allocator for every new card, which also avoids every id the current files still name; every board write writes the `id:` of each card of its subject that lacks one (D5-3, D5-4); a suggested premise keeps its card's id when it is written again (D5-20).
- **Claude reads sentences** (RF-2 d): telelean's turns and tool results show each reference as its target's sentence, rendered at two choke points in the runner; docstring mirrors are written rendered, kept current by board changes and never rewritten inside a session (D5-7, D4-56). Claude never adds a reference, nor gives a deleted target a new declaration (D5-8, D5-21).
- **A frozen card's references hold their targets** (FZ-4 bullet 2, RF-5 a, S12): slice 3's `heldFields` hook is filled, inside its one table and one check, with ES-2's exceptions applied inside it (D5-9); a target that is gone stays held as absent (D5-21); holds never move: a transform that would change what a frozen card refers to waits until that card is unfrozen, deleted too or its reference changed in the window, and what you type holds its own references through your own freeze (D5-25, with slice 4's D4-52 and D4-53); slice 4's `claudeMayChange` gains the reference holds, and in a session nothing that another subject's sentences refer to changes (D5-10, D4-57).
- **Copy reference** (CM-4 row 10), on both views, during a session too (ES-9 bullet 2).
- **What slice 4 left for references:** `#` in the transform window's sentences, Whys and Instructions (TR-8); its Dependencies search opened with `#` (TR-4); other subjects' vocabulary referenced (TR-10 c); new ids from the registry (TR-2 c); TR-13's reference dependents (RF-5 b), with no rewrite: references to a card a transform deletes show “deleted card”, as after the board's Delete (D5-24); the holder rule's `heldFields` hook and `claudeMayChange`'s reference sources (D4-53, D4-57).

**Not in slice 5:** cross-subject card references (D5-6); references in glosses, notes after `---` and Lean fields (D5-11); re-mirroring docstrings inside a session (D5-7, D4-56); rewording references to a deleted card (D5-24); a board-wide find (D5-13); consistency checking (M3).

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Invariants
- **I1.** telelean never gives one card id to two different cards in the project, nor, within a subject, one a card had before, while every registry of the folder is readable (RF-2 b). The same card coming back (Undo, History revert, a premise item written again, D5-20) keeps its id.
- **I2.** A reference names its target only by card id or Lean name; every name shown, or sent to Claude, is computed from the current model, a docstring mirror inside a session aside (D5-7) (RF-2 a, c).
- **I3.** A frozen card's references hold their targets, present or gone, in slice 3's one table and one whole-state check, with ES-2's exceptions applied inside it; refuse, never repair or revalue (RF-5 a). A frozen card's holds never move: a transform that would change what one holds waits until that card is unfrozen, deleted too or its reference changed in the window, and a field the person types holds its own references through their own freeze (D5-25, D4-52, D4-53).
- **I4.** Claude never adds a reference, nor gives a deleted target a new declaration; new tokens come only from the person's text, as telelean recorded it (D5-8, D5-21).
- **I5.** Nothing about references shows anything a hidden Result holds (RV-6): names come from the masked model, candidates from the view's graph, and the person's tokens, session prose and transform drafts included, are validated on the model the person sees.
- **I6.** telelean never rewrites a reference: a token appears or changes only through the person's own edit of the sentence or Why that holds it, and Claude may only keep, reorder or drop one (D5-8, D5-24). A reference to a deleted card shows “deleted card” until the person rewords it.

### 3.2 Ids over time (5a, 5b; D5-3, D5-4, D5-17, D5-20)
- **Recorded.** Before every write of its common write path, ModelWorkspace records the subject's `namedIds` in the model before the write and `namedIdsInText` of each changed file's new text; a session's start records B's; every in-session allocation records its id before the id is written anywhere. Opening a board writes nothing (AP-12). Allocation also avoids every block id of every parsed file and every id the current files name (§2.2), so an id that a `premises:` entry or a token still names is never given before it is recorded; in a session it avoids every id B and the session's records name. What is left unrecorded is an id that existed only between two writes, made by hand, that no file names any more (D5-18).
- **Monotonic.** Undo, Redo and History revert never take an id out (the registry is not on the board stack); a rolled-back step, a declined premise and an Exit leave theirs in; a premise item written again takes its own id back, and so do the new cards of a transform decision's Redo, from its action's ledger `transform` entry (D5-20, §2.2); any other replayed write allocates again and burns its first id.
- **F1.** A derived id depends on the other blocks: with A (`id: cynder-dark`) and an id-less B with the same sentence (derived `cynder-dark-2`), deleting A would make B derive `cynder-dark`, and every reference to A would pass to B. So every plan that changes a model file of a subject also writes the `id:` of every card of that subject that lacks one (`writeSubjectIds`, through the EditSession hook every planner shares), with D3-54's `lose`: the plan that deletes A writes B's `id: cynder-dark-2`. Layout-only plans write none. Sessions already write them (D3-37's `ids` commit, then Apply). The one exception is the board step after Exit or an empty Apply with keep ticks (D3-16): it keeps writing only the kept cards' ids (`planIdWrites` with `cards`), as its confirmation says.
- **No migration.** Nothing is rewritten at upgrade: ids.json appears at the first write or session after slice 5, and a subject's derived ids are written at its first board write, inside that action's Undo step and History entry (the guide says so). Until then, and after that write is undone, a hand deletion can still pass an id to an id-less card with the same sentence (D5-18).

### 3.3 What the person sees (5c1, 5c2, 5f1, 5f2; D5-5, D5-6)
- A token shows as a chip (§4.3) wherever its text is shown as the card's own (the face, "because:", details, review rows, answers, notes, Claude's words), and as its 'plain' rendering everywhere else, through `nameOf`. A label nests its target's own references up to three levels ("…" deeper, or on a cycle).
- A card token resolves only within its subject: another subject's card shows "deleted card" with TL124, a missing one with TL125; telelean's own inputs refuse both (§2.4, §2.7), so they come only from hand edits. A deleted card's id never comes back as another card's (I1), only as itself, by Undo or History revert; a Lean name comes back only through the person's own edit (D5-21; by hand outside a session, D5-18).
- Review rows render both sides with the Proposed (masked) resolver, so a row differs only where the stored text differs; a chip whose target is named differently in your files says so in its tooltip (§2.8).
- In a session, every name, chip and candidate on the Proposed view and in the session's panels comes from the masked model or graph, and `refProblems` (session prose, the premise Accept's Why, slice 4's drafts) runs on the model the person sees: a hand-typed token naming a hidden card is refused exactly as one naming no card.

### 3.4 What Claude reads (5c1, 5c2, 5e; D5-7)
- Every turn and every tool result passes the two choke points (§2.7): the context bundle, step and feedback turns, Shaky notes, "Ask me about" notes, Instructions (S4), answers, Earlier answers from decisions.json, gate findings, freeze texts and thrown tool errors. So Claude reads ‹sentence› [card ID], never a raw token outside a raw span.
- **Raw spans.** Every text that tells Claude a sentence or Why value to keep or restore exactly quotes it in a raw span, so its stored form survives the rendering: G5's quoted sentence and Why lines; slice 3's frozen texts, its FROZEN BY THE PERSON values and `personChangedFreezes`; slice 4's Claude part turns (S4: "the person typed this: keep exactly: ‹value›", "keep exactly: ‹value›", and a cleared field's "it now reads: ‹value›", whose tokens Claude may keep); the feedback turn's "kept: the person froze it" parts; `heldField`'s value; the tokens in the `reference` texts. The wrappers strip the markers after rendering; a marker in the person's own text is dropped first. The person never sees a marker: §2.7's copies go through `renderForPerson`.
- The files Claude reads with its own tools keep the stored form; the system prompt line (§2.8) explains it.
- **Docstrings** (D5-7). telelean writes `mirrorText` wherever it writes a D-9 mirror (normalise, adoption, D-9's follow on the board and on slice 4's direct path, slice 4's typed statements, S4, whose names it resolves on the decision's planned result, §2.6), and normalise requires it only of a docstring that the running step made new or changed, compared with the step's S₀. A docstring written earlier in the session (a statement you typed in a decision, which carries the mirror of its sentence as it was then and which your own freeze covers; an earlier step's statement) is left as it is, like a mirror unchanged since B, even when a later transform retypes its sentence (TR-13 b2 warns) or renames something it refers to: comparing with B would make normalise rewrite a frozen statement, or refuse a docstring Claude may not touch, at every later step (sound-L-08, intent-build-F8). Outside sessions, every board plan that changes a model file of a subject also rewrites each D-9 mirror of that subject whose docstring `isMirrorOf` its sentence but differs from `mirrorText`, in the same Undo step and History entry (a follow-on edit, like F1's ids). The hook reads both on the plan's result, never on the model the plan started from: it re-parses the files the plan changed (markup only, no Lean job), so the resolver sees each card's sentence as the plan leaves it, new and deleted cards included, and rewording a referenced card re-mirrors its referrers in the same plan. A freeze action (`set-freezes`) writes ids only and never re-mirrors (FZ-9: freeze changes change no file; slice 3's id writes, D3-16, and F1's are their one exception). Inside a session no mirror is rewritten (D4-56): a mirror unchanged since B is left as it is, even when a name in it, or its own card's sentence, changed since; only a statement written in the session (Claude's, or one you typed, which carries its sentence's mirror as part of its value) has a new mirror, which equals `mirrorText` when it is written (normalise checks Claude's in the step that writes it) and is then left as it is too.

### 3.5 Claude never adds a reference (5c2; D5-8, D5-21)
For every card block of the session's subject in the judged state, `referenceFindings` checks:
- **`added`:** for `natural_name` and `rationale`, the multiset of tokens lies within B's value's (empty for a new block) plus those of the values telelean recorded for the person on that card and field (D5-8), all read from the ledger:
  - the live `transform` entries' `values` for that card and field (S4, through `liveTransformValues`; new cards through the entry's `ids`): a value that a Reject or take-out dropped never counts, and the Undo of that action, or the un-reject of that Reject, restores it (D4-49, D4-61), so your own tokens are never `added` after you take your Reject back. Drops go by the reverted item, never by owner card (S4: slice 4's §2.2; recheck-02). So a Reject of "Clear proof ‹c›" from the transform that typed c's sentence leaves that sentence's value live, and its tokens allowed; only a revert of `card:<c>` drops them;
  - on a written premise card, the Why its write wrote (the ledger `premise` entry's `rationale`, recorded when the write is planned) and, when the item's draft was edited by the person (S4, D4-58: `editedBy: 'you'`), the sentence its write wrote (the entry's `naturalName`). These count while the entry stands, whatever the item's status: accepted, declined Shaky, cascaded to be redone with its content kept.

  So Claude may keep, reorder or drop the person's tokens, never add, forge or move one.
- **`docstring`:** a declaration of the subject whose text, docstring included, is new or differs from B's holds no token, unless it equals a live `transform` entry's value for that target (a gloss you typed by hand with token-shaped text).
- **`revived`:** a `lean:` token in a sentence or Why of B, of any subject, whose target B lacks and the judged state has, unless a live `transform` entry's value declares that name (a vocabulary declaration you typed, D5-21). A name that a frozen card's reference holds is held as absent (§3.6): that is a `held` finding, sent in `revived`'s words. Your own typed declaration of such a name changes held content, so the window refuses it until that card is unfrozen, deleted too or its reference changed (D5-25), and a forced one is taken back by the decision's judgement (`held`). A name another subject's dangling reference names is `other-subject`'s, and a session's window locks it (D5-10). A name that a `held` or `other-subject` finding already reports is left to it (one message). There is no MUST DEFINE case: typed Lean uses only names that exist (D4-54).
- At step purposes, slice 3's two-value rule applies (§2.4).
- The tools refuse at once: `propose_assumption`'s sentence and `why_needed`, and an `add_card` sentence (slice 3's, if built, and slice 4's in Claude's parts, S4), with §2.8's texts. The check sits beside the tool's other checks, never in `adoptionPlan`, which also writes a premise sentence you edited (D4-58).

### 3.6 References hold their targets (5d; D5-9, D5-10, D5-21, D5-22, D5-25)
- **Holders:** every frozen sentence or Why, whatever its reason. Expected at B (standing, foreign, session-start, own `{ from: 'base' }`): `heldFields` over B. Own freezes and accepted items: their stored held maps, computed by `holdingMap` with the frozen sentence and Why targets as `fields`: own freezes by the runner's `freeze()` and, for a transform decision's own freezes, by slice 4's `valueOwnFreezes` on the decision's judged model (S4, D4-52), each with §2.5's `{ from: 'base' }` test; accepted items through `freezeAtAccept`, with `acceptCovers`' sentence and Why targets. So a sentence or Why you type in a transform holds, from the decision on, what its new words refer to.
- **What is held** (D5-9): `#[card:c]` holds c's sentence; `#[card:c:sentence|why|kind|stance|source]` that field, as text; `#[card:c:statement]` c's statement declarations by their held values (meaning, and text without the docstring) with their meaning closure, or, while c has none, the empty statement (kept empty, FZ-2); `#[card:c:dependencies]` c's Dependencies: no arrow into c removed or added, its mark unchanged (compared by `deps`); for a session target c, without the arrows that are parts of its Results (Claude's arrows into it and its written premises' arrows, what the base mask drops, §2.5), at every valuation and in the check, so a Result's arrow, hidden or not, is never held and its silent removal (a Reject's cascade, a judge's revert, Submit's take-out) is never a `held` finding or a D3-48 row (sound-L-07; slice 3's D3-6 kept a target's Dependencies unfreezable while hidden for the same reason); `#[lean:n]` n's declaration (held value and closure) and its gloss; `#[lean:n:declaration|gloss]` that one; `#[card:c:proof]` nothing (FZ-4: no proof is held). A held sentence or Why holds what its own tokens hold, each target once. A target that is gone is held as absent (D5-21): no card or declaration may take its place.
- **ES-2's exceptions, inside the check** (FZ-4: held never overrides them). `refExceptions` comes, at step purposes, from the running step's inference only (its target, the ends of its selected arrows, and whether its question is its mark), and at `stage`, at `final` and in the backstop from every inference; so a change one step was allowed stays allowed afterwards (at later steps through the two-value rule), and no step gets another inference's exceptions. An arrow added into such a target passes a held Dependencies key, and when that target's question is its mark, the mark may go with it (AR-5: the accepted premise replaces it, as part of the person's Accept); removals are still compared, and so is the mark otherwise. - **The check:** slice 3's step 3 compares every held key, Lean name or field, with `heldValueOf(current, key, results)` (the session's `ResultArrows`, §2.5); its two-value rule, `fresh`, `conflict` and one-message rule are unchanged, since they dispatch by key.
- **Holds never move** (D5-25; D4-53; FZ-3 bullet 2e by replacement; FZ-1). No expectation is ever set aside or revalued. In a session the person changes held content only through a transform, and a transform that would change a key that some frozen card holds (any reason but accepted) is a window problem until that card is unfrozen ([Unfreeze them]), deleted too, or taken into the window with its reference changed (§2.8); forced past the window, it is taken back by the decision's judgement, whose frozen check still compares every holder's expectation (`held`). An accepted holder returns to review through the decision's confirmation (RV-5 bullet 3), its stored map's keys counting like any `frozen.held`. A field you type becomes your own freeze at your value, its standing, foreign and session-start reasons replaced (D4-52): its old words then hold nothing, since `heldFields` walks entries only, and its new words hold through the own freeze's map. A stored map is flat: what it reached through a field that a later transform changes is still held, so that transform finds its holder a problem (an over-hold that fails closed). Outside a session nothing is needed: a standing freeze names a target, not a value, and your board edits are allowed (FZ-1).
- **In a transform** (S4):
  - *Holders.* The analysis finds them in the hold index (§2.5, §2.6): direct ones, those that hold through other held sentences and Whys ("through “‹B›”", from `via`), and those of stored maps. A holder whose holding sentence or Why the window types, or that the window trashes, is none: its own new value replaces it.
  - *Typed holds.* Each sentence and Why the window types (new cards' included) will hold, once the decision values its own freeze, what `referenceHolds` gives for its draft text on the view's model; the analysis adds these planned holds to the index. One on a field the window leaves to Claude (fuzzy, cleared, or a dependent's Lean the write empties) is a problem on the typed field (§2.8): Claude could not change that field. It is given instead of slice 4's held problem on that field when all the field's holders are planned (one message). So is one on a key that a queued Claude part leaves to Claude (its scope's `open` or `arrowsInto`), with §2.8's queued-part text: the decision's own freeze would otherwise take that key from the part silently at Submit review (sound-L-12). One on an open field marks it "held by “‹A›” (it refers to this)" and leaves it out of the analysis's `claude` scope; after the decision the own freeze holds it, so Claude's part leaves it out at Submit review and Retry too (cheat 11).
  - *What Claude may change.* `claudeMayChange` reads the widened index and `otherSubjectRefHolds` (§2.6): a reference-held field, and a Lean statement or vocabulary declaration whose declarations are held names, is out of `open` (and, for Dependencies, out of `arrowsInto`); a fuzzy one is slice 4's held problem; the turn lists it under HELD; the window marks it "held by “‹A›” (it refers to this)".
  - *A dependent left to Claude* (D4-48's "Claude re-formalises it") whose Lean a frozen card's reference holds: the write empties that Lean, which changes held content, so the frozen card is a holder problem like any other; one that a sentence typed in the window refers to is a typed-hold problem.
- **Absent names** (D5-21). A held key expected absent through a reference (a Lean name that a dangling `lean:` token names, held by a frozen card directly or through held sentences and Whys) is sent to Claude with `revived`'s text (`CLAUDE_REF_TEXTS.revived`), never slice 3's held text, and shown to the person with §2.8's `revived` text, under `held`'s code. ## 5. Undo and persistence
- **Board stack:** an edit with references is the same edit-node or add-node step; F1's `id:` lines and the re-mirrors of stale docstrings (D5-7, names as the step leaves them) join that step and its History entry; a freeze action's step writes ids only, never a docstring. ids.json is never on the stack, so Undo, Redo and History revert never shrink it.
